What My ThreatLens does

My ThreatLens helps a cybersecurity analyst monitor selected technologies for relevant vulnerabilities, exploits, and public threat intelligence. It collects matching public-source findings into one temporary review workspace.

How to use the website

  1. Define your scope: on the Dashboard, choose the technologies you use, the security keywords you care about, trusted sources, and a publication date range.
  2. Run the scan: after choosing your scope, select Scan Now and wait for the progress indicator to finish. Your choices are applied when the scan starts.
  3. Update the search: change any technology, keyword, source, or date range, then select Scan Now again to replace the old results with a fresh search.
  4. Watch for zero-days: use Scan for Zero Days for a focused scan of zero-day mentions and active-exploitation reports. Its dedicated results stay separate from the normal Scan Now table. This monitors published intelligence; it does not discover unknown vulnerabilities directly on devices.
  5. Review the results: filter by severity, technology, source, CVE, or review state. Open a finding to read its evidence, email it directly, or ask a finding-specific question.
  6. Email threats: use a finding's Email button for one threat, select checkboxes and choose Email selected, or choose Email all found for every result matching the current filters. Pick a ready-made subject and message or edit your own.
  7. Share the result: Export downloads the currently filtered findings as an Excel workbook. Email sends the filtered report only when the site owner has configured an SMTP mail account.

Browser workspaces and saved data

The site uses one demonstration login, while each browser receives an independent workspace that can contain multiple saved setups. Changes on your laptop do not change the setups in another browser or on another computer.

Setup configuration also has a browser-specific backup that can help restore it after a temporary hosted-server restart. Findings, review state, and chat messages are temporary and are not saved in browser storage.

Email delivery

Email is optional and must be connected by the site owner through the Zoho Mail API or an available SMTP provider. Until then, use Export and attach the generated Excel report through an approved company channel.

Data privacy

My ThreatLens does not collect user email addresses or create personal accounts. The shared demonstration password is stored as a one-way hash, and login sessions use protected browser cookies. SMTP and AI provider secrets stay in server environment settings and are never exposed to users.

Sources

The application is designed for approved government, vendor, and cybersecurity news feeds. The included offline fixture collector keeps tests deterministic.

AI disclosure

The site-wide assistant and each finding-specific assistant use an open AI model through Ollama. AI-assisted summaries, relevance explanations, and chat responses may be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. They are decision-support content, not a substitute for professional judgment. Verify every important claim against the linked primary source, vendor advisory, CVE record, and your organization's actual asset inventory before taking action.

Known limitations

Public sources can change, rate-limit, or become unavailable. This build uses deterministic fixtures by default; live collector validation requires network access.
