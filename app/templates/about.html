Why My ThreatLens?

Traditional vulnerability-management platforms concentrate on discovering assets, inspecting systems, and assessing weaknesses inside managed infrastructure. My ThreatLens solves a different problem: it continuously gathers public security reporting, narrows it to the technologies and risks you select, highlights emerging and actively exploited threats, explains why each finding matters, and turns the evidence into concise emails and reports. It is lightweight, quick to configure, and useful before or alongside internal security scanners. It does not replace asset inventory or technical exposure validation; it adds timely external context, focused prioritization, and clear communication.

01

How to use the website

A complete scan takes four clear steps.

1

Define your scope

Choose the technologies you use, security keywords, trusted sources, and publication date range.

2

Run a scan

Select Scan Now. The progress indicator shows collection and analysis status.

3

Review and prioritize

Filter by severity, technology, source, CVE, or review state. Open a finding to inspect its evidence.

4

Share the result

Email one threat, selected threats, or the filtered report. Export the current view as an Excel workbook.

Important: If you change the scope, run the scan again. Existing results are not silently mixed with the new selection.

03

Zero-day & active-exploitation scan

A separate priority view for urgent published intelligence.

Select Scan for Zero Days to look specifically for explicit zero-day reporting, CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities signals, active exploitation, and public proof-of-concept reporting that matches your scope.

  • Results stay separate from the normal findings table.
  • Metrics distinguish explicit zero-days from active-exploitation signals.
  • Source coverage shows whether the selected feeds answered successfully.
  • A failed source produces an unavailable warning—not a false “safe” conclusion.
What it does not do

It does not scan your devices.

My ThreatLens monitors published intelligence. Confirm exposure with your asset inventory, affected-version checks, vendor guidance, and approved security tools.

04

Accounts, workspaces, and privacy

Independent local accounts backed by the computer's database.

Create a local account

Choose a unique username and strong password. No email address is required.

→

Independent workspace

Every account owns its setups, search parameters, and automatic-email configuration.

→

Setups remain available

Saved setup configuration remains in the local SQLite database after logout, browser closure, or application restart.

Passwords are stored as salted one-way hashes. Provider credentials stay in the local .env file and are never sent to the browser. Local and hosted installations do not synchronize data.

Email delivery

Email uses the administrator's configured Zoho Mail API or SMTP provider. If delivery is unavailable, export the Excel report and share it through an approved channel.

AI disclosure

AI-assisted summaries, relevance explanations, and chat responses may be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect. Verify important claims against the linked primary source, vendor advisory, CVE record, and actual asset inventory.

Testing

Automated unit and API tests cover registration, authentication, account isolation, scan behavior, matching, email formatting, exports, and assistant fallbacks.

What production hosting enables

An always-on paid service turns the demonstration into a more reliable monitoring platform.

⌚

Reliable automation

Run scans every 30 minutes and the daily 9:00 a.m. Beirut scan without keeping a personal laptop or browser open.

✉

Immediate alerts

Email newly discovered Critical threats automatically and retain a delivery history showing what was sent, when, and to whom.

▣

Persistent database

Use managed PostgreSQL so setup automation, scan history, reviews, and alert records survive deployments and server restarts.

↻

Background processing

Move scheduled scans and email jobs to a worker so they continue independently without slowing the website.

◇

Professional access

Add a custom domain, HTTPS, monitoring, backups, improved availability, and enough capacity for more users and sources.

✦

Stronger hosted AI

Connect a dependable hosted model, or use a larger server with sufficient memory—and possibly a GPU—for local model inference.

Recommended production foundation: an always-on web service, managed PostgreSQL, a background worker or scheduled job, and a visible scan and email-delivery history.
07

Use it responsibly

Decision support—not an automatic security verdict.

Source availability: public feeds can change, rate-limit, or become temporarily unavailable.

Coverage: a missing article is not proof that a technology has no vulnerability.

Validation: use primary advisories and your asset inventory before remediation decisions.

AI: treat generated text as an assistant's interpretation, never as the original evidence.
